Experiments in automated award extraction

Can AI produce an award interpretation that a payroll team can trust?

Frontier AI models can give surprisingly good answers to specific entitlement questions. The harder question is whether they can analyse a complete award, preserve every relevant clause, and produce rules that can be reviewed before they are used in payroll.

StatusExperimental process
Primary audienceAssurance and payroll managers
Final artifactAward Interpretation containing rulesets
01

Purpose and executive summary

A plausible AI answer is not enough when people are being paid. A complete interpretation needs to account for the award clauses, make its reasoning traceable, and show where assumptions or omissions have been identified.

I therefore built an end-to-end process rather than asking one model to describe all entitlements in a single response. The process uses multiple AI activities, deterministic checks and human review.

The result is useful in three different ways:

  • Specific entitlement questionsThe model can produce smart, useful answers to focused questions.
  • Award InterpretationThe output is detailed and comprehensive, but can be too convoluted to use directly as a practical payroll ruleset.
  • PseudocodeThe output is deliberately exhaustive. A human would remove much of it, but it is useful for checking that award provisions have not been missed.

The strength is completeness; the weakness is prioritisation. The process is good enough to share, not because it is perfect, but because it asks the right questions and makes potential omissions visible.

02

What the experiment was testing

Could a structured, validated process make AI useful for building award interpretations that can be passed to a software team, used as a secondary check on payroll configuration, or used to investigate what should be paid?

The experiment did not assume that the AI would make the final judgement. It tested whether the surrounding process could expose omissions, preserve evidence and give a human reviewer a practical point at which to challenge the result.

03

The process in one view

Retrieve award→Structure clauses→Classify clauses→Select rulesets→Draft and review→Create Award Interpretation

The Award Interpretation is the final working artifact. It contains the selected rulesets for overtime creation, overtime consequence and penalties. Pseudocode and the calculator questionnaire are produced from that interpretation.

04

How the process is controlled

LLM activities propose clause classifications, interpretations and ruleset drafts.

Deterministic checks check structure, clause coverage, references, duplicates, scope and required fields. They surface differences rather than silently hiding them.

Human review takes place on the consolidated Award Interpretation. The reviewer can simplify or exclude rules while keeping the source references and changes visible.

05

Why the coverage checks matter

Clause 24.1 was identified as relevant to overtime but was not present in the draft ruleset before review. The clause concerns meal breaks: where an employee works during a required break, the time is paid at overtime rates, but remains ordinary time.

This is not an overtime-creation rule. It is a payment rule that belongs in the Award Interpretation because it affects what an employee should be paid.

The deterministic check did not need to interpret the clause. It identified the discrepancy:

Classified as relevant → not represented in the draft ruleset

The reviewer can then decide where the clause belongs and whether the resulting rule is appropriate.

06

What the warnings compare

Each warning compares a stage with its immediate predecessor. This keeps the control meaningful and avoids treating every difference between expert drafts as an error.

StageComparisonWarning
3.1 individual expert2.2 shortlisted clauses vs that expertA shortlisted clause is not represented in the expert draft.
3.1 combinedExpert A or B vs combined rulesetA clause identified by either expert is not represented in the combined ruleset.
3.2 revised ruleset3.1 combined vs 3.2 revisedA clause present before adversarial review is not represented afterwards.

Important: a warning is a review prompt, not proof that the ruleset is wrong. A clause can be excluded deliberately, but the decision should be visible.

07

Detailed step-by-step process

Open a step to see the activity, the AI work, the deterministic controls and the files produced. Human checks are described separately in the process notes.

1Fetch and structure the awardSource record
What happens

Retrieve the award from FWO, or use the supplied PDF. Parse the text into headings, clauses and subclauses, then write the snapshot to structured JSON.

AI activity

N/A. This stage uses code to construct the award structure before interpretation begins.

Deterministic controls

Confirm the source can be accessed, construct the award structure using code, and ensure the outputs can be serialised and written.

Files
Structured award JSON and source snapshot
2.1Classify payment-relevant clausesLLM + deterministic checks
What happens

Segment the award into top-level clause groups and direct subclauses. Classify each clause as payment-relevant, definition-relevant or not relevant. For relevant payment clauses, identify categories including overtime, penalties, allowances, breaks and leave. Omit subclauses that are not relevant from the classified results.

AI activity

An LLM reviews each top-level clause and records whether it is payment- and/or definition-relevant. It reviews each direct subclause to classify the payment type.

Deterministic controls

Check that every top-level clause group supplied to the classifier appears in the output. Check that each direct subclause is classified, marked not relevant or excluded by a documented filtering rule. Reclassify clear missed wording, detect duplicates, and record classifications, exclusions, corrections and edits.

Files
Payment classification JSON
2.2Build the selected ruleset subsetLLM + deterministic checks
What happens

Review each clause for the selected ruleset: overtime or penalties. Within overtime, distinguish clauses that determine when worked time becomes overtime from clauses that apply after overtime has occurred. Identify the employee cohort and work arrangement where the award text supports it.

AI activity

For overtime clauses, an LLM classifies each shortlisted clause by its overtime role and proposes the relevant employee cohort and work arrangement.

Deterministic controls

Confirm every shortlisted clause is classified into an applicable category. Reject duplicate or invented references, validate classification and scope fields, check that cohort and work arrangement are supported by the clause text, and normalise unsupported scope to all. For penalties, apply predefined code rules to select clauses, assign the penalty classification and set the scope.

Files
Ruleset clause-classification JSON
3.1Draft and combine a ruleset within the Award InterpretationLLM activities + checks
What happens

Convert selected clauses into explicit payroll rules designed to be read by a payroll officer. Prepare two independent drafts, compare them and combine them into one draft ruleset component within the Award Interpretation.

AI activity

An LLM runs two independent expert interpretations. Expert A and Expert B each produce a structured ruleset from Step 2.2. A further LLM comparison call proposes how the drafts should be reconciled.

Deterministic controls

Validate both expert rule lists and the comparison output. Confirm that every rule from either expert is accounted for in the comparison and combined ruleset, allowing for similar rules to be combined. Confirm shortlisted clauses remain represented and identify employee-cohort mismatches.

Files
Expert A and B drafts, comparison output and combined ruleset component
3.2Adversarially review the draftLLM evaluator + creator
What happens

Review the Step 3.1 combined ruleset against the clause evidence and classifications from Steps 2.1 and 2.2. The evaluator records rule-by-rule recommendations, including proposed new rules. The creator records decisions and rebuilds the revised ruleset component.

AI activity

An LLM evaluator reviews the combined ruleset against clause evidence. A separate LLM creator records keep, modify, remove and add decisions and produces the revised ruleset.

Deterministic controls

Require every original rule to be addressed. Prevent silent removals and additions, require tracked evaluator and creator records for additions, rebuild from structured decisions, and surface reductions in clause coverage.

Files
Evaluator feedback, creator response, revised ruleset and clause-coverage warnings
4.1Format a reviewed ruleset within the Award InterpretationLLM + coverage warning
What happens

Select the revised ruleset component and transform it into a cleaner, reviewer-facing guide using the appropriate headings and formatting template.

AI activity

An LLM reorganises and rewrites the reviewed component into the required presentation format. This is a formatting activity, not a new interpretation.

Deterministic controls

Compare the reviewed component with the formatted output and flag rules that appear to have been omitted during formatting. Produce warnings rather than automatic repairs.

Files
Formatted ruleset guide and coverage-warning output
4.9Human review of the Award InterpretationHuman review
What happens

Optionally edit the formatted ruleset component and save a separate human-reviewed working copy. The reviewed file becomes the preferred source for Step 5.1.

Files
Human-reviewed Markdown ruleset working file
5.1Generate pseudocode from a reviewed rulesetLLM + rule inventory
What happens

Select the best available reviewed ruleset component, build an inventory of its rules, and generate implementation-oriented pseudocode for technical teams.

AI activity

An LLM translates the reviewed ruleset and rule inventory into pseudocode, required inputs, decision logic and outputs.

Deterministic controls

Compare the pseudocode against the source-rule inventory. Identify missing rule coverage and check required inputs, exclusions, reasons and clause references. Write validation JSON and Markdown reports.

Files
Pseudocode Markdown and validation JSON/Markdown
6.1Generate the calculator questionnaire and Python draftLLM + evidence
What happens

Load the three reviewed rulesets contained in the Award Interpretation—overtime creation, overtime consequence and penalties. Answer a fixed calculator questionnaire and generate a Python draft for the PayGuide calculator.

AI activity

An LLM interprets the three reviewed rulesets to answer the structured questionnaire with supporting evidence, then generates a Python draft from that questionnaire.

Deterministic controls

Validate questionnaire values, normalise the response, retain evidence, identify unsupported or missing fields, and generate Python from the structured questionnaire.

Files
Calculator questionnaire JSON and calculator Python draft
08

Current conclusion

The process is good enough to share. It is not perfect, and it is unlikely that any automated interpretation process will remove the need for judgement in a complex award system. Its value is that it produces useful answers, preserves a broad evidence base, and surfaces the questions that need human attention.
